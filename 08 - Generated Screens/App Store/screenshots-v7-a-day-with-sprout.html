App Store screenshots · V.7 “A day with Sprout”

One idea, built from the research instead of from the last version. Each slide is one real moment in a parent’s day, with a time stamp, a short outcome headline, and the actual Sprout screen for that moment: oversized, no phone frame, one callout on the detail that matters. The first three slides carry the engine Tony described on 5 Nov: school email → to-dos → a reminder your spouse actually saw.

The UI is coded, not exported, so it is sharp at full App Store size and every word can change in seconds.

What the research says

  • The first three slides do the work. They are the only ones shown in search results, and most people never swipe further.
  • Hook → proof → differentiator. Slide 1 says what you get, slide 2 shows it working, slide 3 shows what nobody else does.
  • Real UI, one focus per slide, captions readable at thumbnail size. Benefit first (“your day out”), never a feature list.
  • A set should look like one family: same type, palette and layout, so the row reads as one story.

Sources: AppScreenshotStudio 2026 guide · AppTweak · AppLaunchFlow · MWM · Shotlingo (vendor blogs; treat numbers as directional).

In App Store search

What a parent sees before tapping: the icon and the first three slides at about 104 px wide.

  • Slide 1 is the hook. A real Sprout push with the count, and the two to-dos it pulled from school email. It answers “what is this?” in one glance.
  • Slide 2 is the proof. The Daily Brief itself, big enough to read.
  • Slide 3 is the differentiator. The spouse read receipt (Tony picked it on 5 Nov). No other school app has it.
  • Chat, directory and community come after, for parents who swipe.
