Design decisions — v3

Before → after for each key change across 5.6 and 5.7

Before — 5.6 headline
"Add Your Spouse" — transactional, implies a task to complete
After
"Bring them along" + italic — warm, invitational, feels like an offer not a form
Before — 5.6 body copy
3-line paragraph: "Enter their phone number. Once they join Sprout, your family profile — location, kids, and school groups — will be shared…"
After
1 line: "Enter their number and we'll text an invite." — The card already shows what's shared; the paragraph was redundant
Before — shared data card header
"Shared with your spouse" with a warning circle icon — reads like a privacy disclaimer
After
"Everything you set up, ready for them" with a leaf icon — frames it as a gift, not a disclosure
Before — data row values
Plain text: "Jesica, Liam, Sophia" / "Pre-K, Kinder" — all the same weight, easy to skim past
After
Chips for each name/grade — scannable at a glance, reinforces the richness of what was set up
Before — 5.6 bottom buttons
Skip | Send Invite — equal-weight pill buttons compete, dilutes the primary action
After
Full-width "Send Invite" CTA + "Skip for now" as small text link — one clear primary, skip is still reachable but not competing
Before — 5.7 celebration
Flat green circle with checkmark — generic, no warmth or personality
After
Botanical leaf burst + ringed check circle — matches Sprout's nature identity, feels earned/celebratory
Before — 5.7 two separate cards
"Invite Sent To" card + "Your spouse will have access to" card — two similar-looking cards, fragmented
After
Inline sent-to row + single "Here's what they'll get" card — hierarchy is clear: recipient first, then what they receive
Before — 5.7 wrong number
"Wrong Number? — Tap to Re-Enter" as a full secondary button — takes up prime real estate for an edge case
After
"Entered the wrong number?" as an underlined text link — still accessible, doesn't compete with Explore Sprout CTA
